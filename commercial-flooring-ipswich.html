Floors specified for how the space is used

A reception sees thousands of footsteps a week. A care home corridor has to be cleanable and slip resistant. An office floor has to cope with chair castors and let engineers reach cabling under raised floors. We start by asking how each space is used, then specify a product rated for it.

Commercial flooring has to meet standards that domestic flooring does not. Commercial flooring in more detail

Use classes and wear ratings

Commercial flooring is graded by how much traffic it can handle. Products rated for heavy commercial use have thicker wear layers and denser construction than domestic ranges. We match the rating to each area, so a busy reception gets a heavier product than a quiet meeting room and the budget goes where it matters.

Slip resistance

In kitchens, washrooms, entrances and clinical areas, slip resistance is a safety requirement. Safety flooring is tested for slip potential in wet and dry conditions. We recommend products with sustained slip resistance, meaning the performance lasts for the life of the floor instead of wearing off.

Moisture in concrete slabs

New concrete takes months to dry, and older slabs can draw damp from the ground. Flooring adhesives fail when moisture is too high, causing tiles to lift and bubbles to form. We test with a hygrometer before fitting and use a surface damp-proof membrane where the readings call for it.

Acoustics in offices

Hard floors in open-plan offices can make them noisy. Carpet tiles absorb sound and reduce footfall noise, which helps in shared workspaces. Where you want a hard floor, an acoustic-backed LVT reduces impact noise to the floor below.

Hygiene in healthcare and food areas

Clinical rooms, care homes and commercial kitchens need floors without gaps where dirt and bacteria collect. Sheet safety flooring with heat-welded seams and coved skirting that runs up the wall creates one continuous, easy-clean surface.

Lifetime cost

The cheapest floor to install is rarely the cheapest over its life. A heavier carpet tile or LVT may cost more upfront but last years longer and look better for longer. We can give you options at different price points with their expected life, so you can compare properly.

What affects the cost of commercial flooring

Commercial projects are priced from a survey. These factors make the biggest difference.

Floor area and layout
Large open areas are quicker per square metre than lots of small rooms.
Product specification
Heavier use classes, safety flooring and acoustic backing cost more.
Subfloor works
Levelling, damp-proof membranes and removing old adhesive add time and materials.
Working hours
Night and weekend work can cost more than standard hours.
Phasing
Splitting a job into stages adds visits but keeps the business running.

Frequently asked questions

Can you fit outside our opening hours?

Yes. Most commercial installs happen in the evening, overnight or at weekends, or are phased room by room so part of the building stays open.

Which flooring is best for an office?

Carpet tiles are the usual choice. They absorb noise, cope with chair castors and lift individually for access to floor boxes. Kitchens, washrooms and break-out areas are usually better in LVT or safety vinyl.

Do you test concrete floors for moisture?

Yes, on every slab we fit over. High moisture is the most common cause of commercial flooring failure, so we test first and use a damp-proof membrane where the readings call for one.

Can you work to our architect's or facilities team's specification?

Yes. We can price and fit to an existing specification, or suggest equivalent products if lead times or budget are an issue.

Can you remove old commercial flooring?

Yes. We lift and dispose of old carpet tiles, vinyl and LVT, and scrape or grind off old adhesive so the new floor bonds properly.

Do you provide RAMS and insurance documents?

Yes. Risk assessments, method statements and insurance details are available before work starts, so your facilities or compliance team can sign off the job.

How long does commercial flooring last?

It depends on the product and the traffic. A good carpet tile in an office often lasts many years, and commercial LVT and safety vinyl can last longer with proper cleaning. We give you cleaning and maintenance guidance to get the most from the floor.

Do you work with letting agents and landlords?

Yes. We fit between tenancies across Ipswich, with quick turnarounds and hard-wearing products chosen to last several tenancies.
